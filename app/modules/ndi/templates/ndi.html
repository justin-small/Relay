    Watch the sources you rely on

    Relay can keep an eye on the sources the booth needs during a service, and flash the stage alert button named “NDI” (make one on the Alerts page) when one goes offline, drops frames, goes silent or clips. A problem has to last the hold time first, so a blip raises nothing. While it is watching, Relay holds a small low-bandwidth stream of each source to measure it.

    Analysis

    Why a feed is stuttering, without a separate tool: how steadily its frames arrive, the picture on scopes, and the sound's levels and loudness. Relay receives the source while this page is open.

    Choose a source and press Analyse.

    A still picture, such as a slide, sends no frames, so a gap there is not a fault.

    Frame timing

    Late is a gap over twice the frame time. A gap over 150 ms is always flagged. The data rate is the decoded picture, not what crossed the network.

    {# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Screens #}

    Multiviews

    Several sources, clocks and countdowns on one screen, for a booth monitor or a stage TV. Open its link in a browser on this network. Relay draws it once, however many screens watch.

    Multiview

    Drag a source, clock or countdown onto a box. Or click a box, then click what it should show.

    One source on its own

    Every source has its own page that plays it full screen, with its name, an audio meter and the tally border. Open it on a booth laptop, a phone or a TV on this network. The Watch buttons on Coming in open it.

    {# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Going out #}

    Switch what TVs show

    Each row is an NDI feed Relay sends, named Relay - <name>. Set a TV or decoder to receive it once, then click a square to change what it shows. Nothing is re-encoded, and the choices come back when Relay restarts.

    No feeds yet. Add one for each TV you want to switch from here.

    Presets

    Save every feed's choice at once and bring it back with one click, such as Sunday morning.

    Decoders such as the Magewell Pro Convert cannot be switched from here: point them at one of these feeds instead.

    Relay pages as NDI

    Turn on a caption screen or overlay and it appears as an NDI source for your switcher, ProPresenter, OBS or vMix. Overlays keep their transparency. Each one draws the page in a hidden browser and uses some of this computer's processor, so turn on only what you use.

    1920 x 1080 is sharper on big screens and uses about twice the processor.
    5 to 30. Captions look fine at 15.

    Test patterns

    Colour bars, a grid, a moving bar, a tone or your own picture, sent as NDI sources so you can line up projectors, TVs, switchers and sound before a service. Each shows up in the router like any other source, so you can send bars to every lobby TV at once. The name, size and a running clock are written on the picture.
