Dante

The Dante devices Relay finds on its Dante network: stage boxes, Dante Virtual Soundcard and Dante Via. Each appears once by its Dante name, with the transmit channels it announces and whether its control port answers. The routing matrix below can change which channel a receive channel listens to; everything else on this page only looks. Renaming, gain, sample rate, latency, clock leader and AES67 mode stay in Dante Controller. Works with Dante devices.

A device that is alive (it sends its multicast heartbeat) but whose control port is silent is shown as such. The heartbeat does not cross a router, so from the main network only the control port tells. Between the main network and a Dante VLAN, the router has to let UDP through to the control port (4440 for most devices, 24440 for Dante Via).

Routing

Receive channels down, transmit channels across. A dot is a live subscription; click a cell to subscribe that receive channel to that transmit channel, or click the dot to clear it. Every change asks first, names what it replaces, and can be undone. Changing a subscription is untested on real Dante gear: Relay reads the device back after each change and says plainly when it cannot confirm it.

A locked receive channel (the house PA feed, say) is never changed by Relay, not by a change here and not by a preset. An administrator locks and unlocks channels with the lock button. A network under Dante Domain Manager refuses changes unless Relay has its credentials, which Relay does not support yet; the refusal is shown as it comes.
